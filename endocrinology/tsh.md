# Clinical Guideline: Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH) Evaluation

- **Category:** Endocrinology / Preventive Health
- **Target Audience:** General Practitioners, Preventive Health Clinicians
- **Last Reviewed:** 2026-09-18
- **Lead Author:** Dr. Tom
- **Status:** Active Master Document

---

## 1. Clinical Overview

Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH) is the primary first-line screening biomarker for evaluating thyroid function. Subclinical thyroid dysfunction frequently presents with vague, non-specific symptoms such as fatigue, weight changes, and mood lability.

Testing should be performed in a fasting state in the morning whenever possible, as diurnal variation can cause mild afternoon dips in TSH values.

---

## 2. Reference Thresholds

| Clinical Category | TSH Range (mIU/L) | Interpretation & Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Overt Hyperthyroidism** | < 0.1 | Suppressed. Check free T4/T3. Urgent referral or endocrine workup. |
| **Subclinical Hyperthyroidism** | 0.1 – 0.39 | Low normal / suppressed. Retest in 6–8 weeks with thyroid antibodies. |
| **Optimal / Normal Range** | 0.4 – 2.5 | Normal thyroid physiology. No clinical intervention indicated. |
| **Borderline High** | 2.6 – 4.2 | Upper conventional reference range. Consider Anti-TPO if symptomatic. |
| **Subclinical Hypothyroidism** | 4.3 – 9.9 | Elevated. Repeat test with free T4 and Anti-TPO before starting therapy. |
| **Overt Hypothyroidism** | ≥ 10.0 | High. Initiate levothyroxine therapy per weight-based dosing protocol. |

---

## 3. Investigation Protocol

1. **Initial Abnormal Screen:**
   - If TSH is between `4.3 – 9.9 mIU/L` with normal free T4, do not start replacement immediately unless the patient is pregnant or symptomatic with high positive Anti-TPO titers.
   - Repeat test in **8 to 12 weeks** to rule out transient thyroiditis.

2. **Follow-Up Monitoring:**
   - For patients initiated on Levothyroxine, recheck serum TSH **6 to 8 weeks** after dosage adjustments until stable.

---

## 4. Exclusion Criteria & Caveats

- **Severe Non-Thyroidal Illness:** In acutely ill or hospitalized patients, TSH can be falsely suppressed or elevated ("euthyroid sick syndrome").
- **Biotin Interference:** High-dose biotin supplements (> 5 mg/day) can cause falsely low TSH results on standard immunoassays. Advise patients to hold biotin for 48 hours prior to phlebotomy.
